The Land & Its People

Coast Salish Nations

The territories of the Musqueam, Squamish, and Tsleil-Waututh Nations have been home to their peoples since time immemorial. Today, these Nations lead some of the most transformative development projects in Canada.

Musqueam

xʷməθkʷəy̓əm "Place of the məθkʷəy̓ (river grass)"

The Musqueam people have lived in the Fraser River delta for over 4,000 years, making them one of the oldest continuous communities in the region. With over 1,300 members today, their traditional territory spans much of what is now Metro Vancouver — from the North Shore mountains to the mouth of the Fraser River, covering present-day Vancouver, Burnaby, Richmond, Delta, and Coquitlam.

Musqueam are renowned for their Coast Salish weaving traditions and their deep ancestral connection to the river and coastline. Archaeological sites at c̓əsnaʔəm (the Marpole Midden), one of the most significant heritage sites in BC, demonstrate thousands of years of continuous habitation and a rich cultural history centered on fishing, trade, and ceremony.

Today, the Musqueam Indian Band operates from their main reserve along the north arm of the Fraser River, near the University of British Columbia. They are active leaders in cultural revitalization, language preservation (hən̓q̓əmin̓əm̓), urban development, and Indigenous economic partnerships across the Lower Mainland.

Squamish Nation

Sḵwx̱wú7mesh Úxwumixw

The Squamish Nation's territory stretches across 6,732 square kilometres of land and water — from the Burrard Inlet to the Squamish Valley, Howe Sound, and the Whistler area. Archaeological sites confirm over 8,600 years of continuous habitation, making it one of the longest-documented Indigenous presences in British Columbia.

The modern Squamish Nation was formed in 1923 through the amalgamation of several Coast Salish bands who had historically shared language, kinship, and territory. Today the Nation has approximately 4,200 members and operates from multiple reserves across the Lower Mainland and Sea-to-Sky corridor, including prominent urban reserves in North Vancouver and West Vancouver.

Squamish Nation is one of the most active First Nations in urban development and cultural preservation in Canada. The Sḵwx̱wú7mesh sníchim (Squamish language), though critically endangered, is at the centre of an intensive revitalization effort, and the Nation's cultural programs draw from millennia of oral history, art, and ceremony.

Tsleil-Waututh Nation

səlilwətaɬ "People of the Inlet"

The Tsleil-Waututh people have called the Burrard Inlet home for over 4,000 years. Their territory encompasses the lands and waters surrounding the inlet — the waterway that defines Vancouver's northern edge. With approximately 600 members today, the Nation is centred on the north shore of the inlet near the community of Deep Cove.

Tsleil-Waututh are widely recognized for their leadership in environmental stewardship. Their Sacred Trust initiative and Burrard Inlet Action Plan have set the standard for Indigenous-led environmental monitoring and restoration, working to bring the health of the inlet's waters and ecosystems back to pre-contact conditions. The Nation played a key role in the legal challenge against the Trans Mountain Pipeline expansion.

In 2025, Tsleil-Waututh signed a historic Reconciliation Agreement with the Government of Canada — a landmark in Crown-First Nations relations that addresses both past wrongs and future partnership. The Nation continues to pursue economic self-sufficiency while maintaining an unwavering commitment to environmental responsibility.

The MST Development Corporation is a historic partnership between the Musqueam, Squamish, and Tsleil-Waututh Nations. Together they hold a portfolio of over 160 acres of prime Metro Vancouver land — one of the most significant Indigenous-led real estate portfolios in Canada.
